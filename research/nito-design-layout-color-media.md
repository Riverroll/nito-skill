# Rebuild nito-design on evidence, not assertion

The skill's layout and colour guidance is Apple's house style presented as measurement, and the published evidence supports only a small part of it. The firmest ground is normative: WCAG 2.2 gives checkable numbers for contrast (4.5:1 text, 3:1 large text and UI), reflow at 320 CSS px, 200% text resize, text-spacing robustness and target size (24 px at AA, 44 px at AAA). Real systems (SAP, Material, Fluent, Apple's HIG, Carbon, GOV.UK) converge on adaptive patterns that the skill's "below 1069px is a different product" ERP line contradicts. The baseline audit also found defects that need no research to fix: the shipped `--ink-muted` fails the audit's own 4.5:1 rule, one dark-mode contrast figure is wrong (`#06c` is 5.57:1 on white, not 17.3:1), alpha ink is never composited, and prose, audit and tests disagree on thresholds. Most "design laws" it leans on (F and Z patterns, 8-point grids, 12 columns, golden ratio, 7-item menus, 60-30-10, red buttons convert, "never pure black") have no empirical support in the notes and must be labelled convention. Evidence on enterprise screens used in the field is thin: no controlled study of responsive ERP layouts, glove sizes, sunlight legibility or offline indicators was opened, so those become declared-condition prompts and tests, not numeric rules. For media, Claude cannot generate raster images ([Anthropic vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), so the skill needs a brief-to-asset ladder: real assets first, then code-authored SVG, CSS, Canvas and rendered UI scenes, then programmatic video, then user-keyed generation APIs, with provenance and honesty rules. The prioritised change list is at the end of the report.

Evidence labels used throughout: STD (normative standard), PR (peer-reviewed), IND (industry research, methods partly undisclosed), DOC (vendor documentation or source code, which describes practice and is not an outcome study), OBS (read from live CSS on 2026-11-16), CONV (convention with no empirical basis found), SECONDARY or UNVERIFIED (seen only in a summary or snippet, never to be turned into a rule). Line references to the repo come from a baseline audit made at commit `d9dda93`; the two rename commits since then touched 15 lines across 24 files, so the references still hold. Paths below are under `/Users/devmaa/nito-skill/skills/nito-design/` unless stated.

## The skill's own numbers contradict each other and cannot be reproduced

The skill opens with "Every number here was measured from apple.com's own shipped stylesheets" ([SKILL.md:8](file:///Users/devmaa/nito-skill/skills/nito-design/SKILL.md)), yet the repo ships no raw data for spacing or colour, only `type-scale.json` for typography. The baseline audit sorted the rules into three groups. Some rules carry a stated measurement basis whose data is not in the repo (the 38/51/12/6% media split from eight pages, the 739-stylesheet corpus). Others carry no basis at all: the dark alt-surface band "1.05-1.15:1", the "12:1" on-dark ceiling, hairline alpha caps, scrim stops, every ERP geometry value except the type ladder, and the audit thresholds, which cannot be "measured on apple.com" because the author chose them. "8% of men cannot separate the success green from the warning amber" has no source in the repo. An independent read of apple.com's live CSS on 2026-11-16 gives a mixed verdict. The breakpoints reproduce (734, 735-1068, 1069 and 1441 px; `max-width:1068px` appears 31 times, 734px 20 times in `main.built.css`), as do 17px body text, `#1d1d1f` and `#f5f5f7` surfaces and the `#0071e3` button ([OBS: main.built.css](https://www.apple.com/v/homepage/a/styles/main.built.css)). The 1068 px "content column" does not: the iPhone page sets `--global-content-max-width: 1260px` (hero 1680px, 420px on phone) on a 12-column grid with a 24px column gap, and its section padding variables are 144/120/112 px and 120/80/56 px, not the skill's 80/100/60 ladder ([OBS: iPhone overview.built.css](https://www.apple.com/v/iphone/home/cl/built/styles/overview.built.css)). Only two pages were read and the padding-to-breakpoint mapping was inferred from file order, so the right conclusion is "unreproduced on the evidence available", not "wrong". The skill should say that.

The colour defects are arithmetic. `--ink-muted: #86868b` on `#fff` is 3.62:1, and `.t-caption` sets 12px text in it, while the audit fails text under 4.5:1 below 24px and `conformance.mjs` only requires 3:1 for muted ink ([apple-base.css:25,115](file:///Users/devmaa/nito-skill/skills/nito-design/assets/apple-base.css), [conformance.mjs:156](file:///Users/devmaa/nito-skill/test/conformance.mjs)). The audit's figure and a separate computation from apple.com's CSS agree on 3.62:1, and the same computation shows Apple's own secondary text grey `#6e6e73` passes at 5.07:1 on white and 4.66:1 on `#f5f5f7` (OBS plus own computation from the same stylesheet, [main.built.css](https://www.apple.com/v/homepage/a/styles/main.built.css)). So Apple uses the lighter grey only as a tertiary tier, and the skill's token mapping gives it to captions. `dark.md` says `#06c` is "17.3:1" on white and "1.2:1" on black; the baseline audit computes 5.57:1 and 3.77:1 (the 5.57:1 is reproduced independently from apple.com's CSS), so the sentence "gone on black" rests on a wrong number ([dark.md:95-102](file:///Users/devmaa/nito-skill/skills/nito-design/references/dark.md)). The same file says the dark alt surface sits 1.05-1.15:1 from base while shipping `#000` with `#1d1d1f` (1.25:1, exactly at the conformance ceiling), and says body ink should stay under about 12:1 while the dark default `#f5f5f7` on `#000` is 19.3:1. The skill's alpha-ink tokens (`rgb(245 245 247 / 0.42)`) are read by the audit at full-channel colour, so a step that is really 3.64-3.85:1 is judged at roughly 16-19:1, and `restraint.mjs` then asserts "nothing fails WCAG AA" on a page that uses that token on 12px text. The audit never emulates dark scheme, and `conformance.mjs` pins `prefers-color-scheme: light`, so every dark-mode number in `dark.md` is untested.

Layout has its own contradictions. The section-padding ladder lists ten values while the restraint alarm fires at four distinct values and the showcase page uses 220, 128, 96, 88 and 56px, mostly off the ladder. The ERP "six-value" spacing list (4/8/12/16/24/32) conflicts with its own 6px help gap and "20-24px" gutter, and the audit enforces only `% 4`, so 6px is flagged while 20, 28 and 36 pass even though the message says only six values are allowed. `.btn--sm` is 24px at compact density against a 28px hit-target floor. The 28px title "ceiling" is flagged by the audit only above 32px. The tablet column is a fixed 734px in CSS but `calc(50% - min(1068px, 87.5%)/2)` in `micro.md`. The 22px gutter is "phone only" in prose and applied at every width in CSS. Dead-screen thresholds read 25% (prose), 35% (code) and "about one in five" (message); scale contrast reads x6, "under x4.5", 4.5 in code, "about x4" in the message and 5 in a test. Typography numbers feeding the hierarchy rules (a 700-weight 56px step, a 72px size, "crosses zero at 40px") do not appear in `type-scale.json`, which contains no weight 700 and no 72px entry. The ERP profile's rule that a "different product" starts below 1069px has no support in any vendor source read, as shown below.

The coverage gaps matter as much as the defects. Confirmed by grep, the skill has no rule on line length, touch targets outside one ERP sentence, colour-blind safety beyond that one unsourced line, semantic state colours outside ERP (and no hex values for them in guidance), focus indicators in the base CSS, non-text contrast, data-visualisation colour, colour spaces, `prefers-contrast`, `forced-colors`, `color-scheme`, scanning order, horizontal grid beyond the 3/2/1 card grid, marketing forms or tables, or any container-based behaviour. The audit probes widths 1440, 900 and 500 only, runs ERP probes only at 1440, and has no check for ladder membership, gutter, column behaviour, reflow, zoom or text-spacing overrides.

## Evidence supports a few layout rules and labels the popular ones as convention

**Line length.** The skill has no measure rule. The empirical record (1983-2006, CRT-era, 1024x768) says long lines are read as fast or faster than short ones, readers prefer moderate or short lines, and comprehension is mostly flat; the speed figures come from a secondary newsletter summary of Duchnicky and Kolers, Dyson and Kipping, Youngman and Scharff and others ([Human Factors International summary, SECONDARY](https://humanfactors.com/newsletters/optimal_line_length.html)). Shaikh 2005 (20 participants, 35/55/75/95 characters per line) found 95 fastest and 35 slowest with no comprehension effect, but that is from a search summary ([HFES record, SECONDARY](https://portfolio.erau.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-line-length-on-reading-performance-of-online-news-/)). The only normative number is WCAG 1.4.8 at Level AAA, "no more than 80 characters or glyphs (40 if CJK)", and its Understanding page cites no study for 80 ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#visual-presentation)). The popular 45-75 or 66 "ideal" is Bringhurst-era typographic convention that the notes could not open. Real systems cluster at 60-75: GOV.UK says "no more than 75 characters per line", Baymard says 50-75 with `max-width` near 70ch, and a Material codelab says 60 ([GOV.UK layout](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/layout/), [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability), [Android codelab](https://developer.android.com/codelabs/adaptive-material-guidance)). USWDS publishes six measure tokens from 44ex to 88ex and recommends none ([USWDS measure](https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-tokens/typesetting/measure/)). Strength: MODERATE that very short lines (about 25-35 cpl) read slower, WEAK for any single optimum. The skill should add a cap on prose at 80 characters, derived from the AAA criterion and named as such, with a default of about 65-75ch labelled as a preference-and-speed compromise and design-system convention. `apple-base.css` sets no `max-width` on any text class today, and the showcase page uses ad hoc `ch` and `rem` values with no rule behind them.

**Line height and text spacing.** WCAG 1.4.12 (AA) requires that nothing breaks when a user forces line height to 1.5, paragraph spacing to 2, letter spacing to 0.12 and word spacing to 0.16 times the font size; those are robustness thresholds, not recommended defaults ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#visual-presentation)). Empirical support for any particular ratio is thin: Ling and van Schaik 2007 found double spacing and left alignment faster for visual search while participants preferred justified text (SECONDARY, [Teesside record](https://research.tees.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-line-spacing-and-text-alignment-on-visual-search/)), and the extra-large letter-spacing benefit for dyslexic children (Zorzi 2012) supports user-adjustable spacing, not a universal default ([Padova page](https://ccnl.psy.unipd.it/publications/publications_folder/extra-large-letter-spacing-improves-reading-in-dyslexia/view)). The skill's tight display leading and em rhythm ladder is craft convention. Nothing in the notes contradicts it, but nothing supports it, and 1.4.12 is the only thing a test can enforce.

**Whitespace and "premium".** Coursaris and Kripintris 2012 varied an e-commerce site at 25, 50 and 75% white space and reported perceived usability falling above about 50%, with a warning against cutting content to create space (single study, abstract level, [IGI Global](https://www.igi-global.com/article/content/62277)). The luxury-whitespace papers (Pracejus and others) were confirmed to exist only, so no finding is claimed. The skill's "negative space is around the composition" and 100px-above-headline numbers are Apple craft; present them as a marketing-page positioning choice and keep dense tools on separate budgets, which both the Coursaris result and the density literature support.

**Scanning and hierarchy.** NN/g's F-pattern comes from eye tracking of 232 users and is a failure mode on poorly structured text: NN/g's own 2019 ranking puts it worst of four patterns and recommends headings and bullets ([NN/g 2006](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/), [NN/g 2019](https://nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/)). Attention data from 120 participants and over 130,000 fixations put 57% of viewing time above the fold and 74% within two screenfuls in 2018, and 80% of fixations on the left half of general pages ([NN/g scrolling](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/), [NN/g left bias](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-attention-leans-left/)); methods are partly undisclosed and the sample is left-to-right-language readers. The Z-pattern has no empirical basis in the notes and is a heuristic derived from the print Gutenberg diagram. Common region (shared container) is a real grouping principle (Palmer 1992, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1516361/)), and applied models show grouping and local density predict search time while alignment mainly predicts preference (Parush 1998, 2005, SECONDARY, [Penn State record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/the-impact-of-visual-layout-factors-on-performance-in-web-pages-a/)). The skill should therefore say: put the primary item top and left, use headings and bullets so text is not read in an F, group with region or consistent proximity, and never describe an F or Z layout as a template. Its own "everything centred is a tell" rule is fine as craft but has no study behind it; left alignment for dense text is consistent with the line-length and scanning evidence.

**Grids and scales.** No empirical study of 8-point spacing, 12-column grids or modular type scales was found; the golden-ratio preference has a mixed and partly negative history (Fechner 1876 to Phillips and Norman 2010, SECONDARY, [PDF](https://www-prod01.wku.edu/psychological-sciences/labs/vision_and_haptics/downloads/phillips_norman_aesthetics2010.pdf)). Documented systems do converge on a 4 or 8px base (Carbon, USWDS, Atlassian 8; Fluent, Ant 4) while GOV.UK uses 5px steps, so the skill's 4px ERP list is defensible as a consistency device, and the notes recommend saying exactly that ([Carbon spacing](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/spacing/overview/), [USWDS spacing](https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-tokens/spacing-units/), [GOV.UK spacing](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alphagov/govuk-frontend/main/packages/govuk-frontend/src/govuk/settings/_spacing.scss)). Wording like "anything not on this list is a mistake" claims more than convention allows.

**First impressions.** Appeal judgements form within 50 ms and track visual complexity and prototypicality (Lindgaard 2006, Tuch 2012, Reinecke 2013, [Tuch et al.](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/), [Reinecke et al.](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2013/reinecke13predicting.shtml)). Low complexity plus high prototypicality rates best, which sits in tension with distinctive hero layouts; the skill should name that trade-off. "Beautiful therefore usable" is contested (Hassenzahl and Monk reviewed 15 papers showing "remarkably high variability", SECONDARY, [UXPA](https://uxpajournal.org/?p=4392)).

**Cognitive limits.** Miller's seven is about memory span for arbitrary items and NN/g says menus need not be capped at seven; the choice-overload record conflicts (a 2010 meta-analysis of N = 5,036 found a mean effect of virtually zero, a 2015 meta-analysis of N = 7,202 found effects once moderators are included) ([Scheibehenne et al.](https://edoc.unibas.ch/48789), [Chernev et al.](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual)). The skill should cite chunking and recognition over recall and never "7 items". The notes also name items that must not be cited at all: "Lin 2004" and "Chaparro et al. 2004" could not be identified, Tullis's R-squared figures are from a search summary, the Dyson and Haselgrove "55 cpl" figure is from a blog, and Bringhurst was not opened.

## ERP screens that work anywhere need adaptive patterns, pointer-keyed density and tested field states

**What the skill says versus what vendors do.** `erp.md` states "one band that matters: >=1069px. Below that, a different product" ([erp.md:37](file:///Users/devmaa/nito-skill/skills/nito-design/references/erp.md)), sets a 28px hit-target minimum with "not 44, that is the touch figure", 14px body, 40px default rows and a 260px sidebar, and has no phone layout, no touch density, no offline state and no responsive table rule. No vendor source opened uses 1069. SAP's UI5 ranges are Phone under 600, Tablet 600-1023, Desktop 1024-1439 and LargeDesktop 1440 and up ([openui5 Device.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAP/openui5/master/src/sap.ui.core/src/sap/ui/Device.js)); Material window classes are Compact under 600 dp, Medium 600-839, Expanded 840-1199, Large 1200-1599, Extra-large 1600 and up ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/use-window-size-classes)); Workday Canvas uses 320, 768, 1024, 1440 ([canvas-kit](https://unpkg.com/@workday/canvas-kit-react@14.0.6/dist/es6/common/lib/theming/breakpoints.js)); Odoo's `isSmall` flag is true below 768 ([odoo 18.0](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/18.0/addons/web/static/src/core/ui/ui_service.js)); Fluent 2 has six classes from 320 to 1920 and names five techniques (reposition, resize, reflow, show/hide, re-architect) ([Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout)). Boundaries recur near 600, 768, 1024 and 1440, but Carbon (672/1056/1312/1584) and Ant (576/992/1200/1600) diverge, and real sites most often use 768, 992, 1024 and 1200 ([Web Almanac 2022 CSS](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2022/css)). That is convergence of vendor practice, not evidence that any band set is better, and `stacks.md` calling Tailwind's 640/768/1024/1280 "wrong" overreaches. Apple's own HIG adds that layout is chosen by size class, not device or orientation, and that an app should not change functionality with the space it occupies, only how much is visible ([HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout.json)). Both HIG and Android say size classes change at runtime (split screen, foldables) and warn against `isTablet` logic, which favours container-width behaviour. SAP already does this for padding (1rem at 600px or less, 2rem above, 3rem at 1440 and up, by container width) ([UI5 docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAP-docs/sapui5/main/docs/04_Essentials/enabling-responsive-paddings-according-to-the-control-width-3b718b5.md)), and container queries and the other tools are now Baseline-class: 95.96% global support for size container queries, 96.07% for `svh/lvh/dvh`, 97.35% for `env()` and 97.39% for `pointer`/`hover` ([caniuse data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Fyrd/caniuse/main/features-json/css-container-queries.json)).

**Density keyed to input, not width.** SAP documents the one rule in the notes that ties density to capability: touch devices get cozy for the whole app (44px touch area), mouse and keyboard devices get compact (rows and toolbars 32px), hybrids choose with a user setting, and cozy and compact must not be mixed within a hierarchy; the 44/32 numbers were read on a Wayback snapshot of SAP's old page, while a second note could only see them in search snippets, so cite them as read from the archived page ([SAP cozy/compact, Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://experience.sap.com/fiori-design-web/cozy-compact/), [UI5 content densities](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAP-docs/sapui5/main/docs/04_Essentials/content-densities-e54f729.md)). AWS Cloudscape says comfortable is always the default, compact is an opt-in for data-intensive views, it is not applied to help panels, alerts, validation messages, dropdowns or data visualisation, and it is not applied to single pages ([Cloudscape](https://cloudscape.design/foundation/visual-foundation/content-density/)); Carbon exposes five row heights (24, 32, 40 default, 48, 64) with header and toolbar matching ([Carbon data table](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/data-table/usage/)). The skill's `--row-h` token already implements the mechanism; it lacks the trigger. The browser primitive exists: `pointer` and `any-pointer` (coarse, fine) and `hover` are Baseline widely available since December 2018, with the caveat that `pointer` tests only the primary device, so hybrids need `any-pointer` ([MDN pointer](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/pointer), [MDN any-pointer](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/any-pointer)). No source opened says how real touch laptops report these values.

**Target size.** The numbers come from different questions and the skill should stop blending them. WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) requires 24x24 CSS px with a spacing exception, and the Working Group chose 24 from the average target size of high-traffic sites, not from fingertip research; 2.5.5 (AAA) asks 44x44 ([Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), [Understanding 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)). Apple's HIG gives iOS 44x44 pt default with 28x28 pt minimum and macOS 28x28 default with 20x20 minimum, plus about 12 pt of padding around bezelled elements ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.json)); Material says 48 dp (about 9 mm) with 8 dp spacing ([Google](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)); Fluent says 44 for iOS and web and 48 for Android. Research gives physical sizes: Parhi, Karlson and Bederson 2006 recommend 9.2 mm for discrete and 9.6 mm for serial one-handed thumb tasks on 2006 handhelds ([MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=155150)), NN/g says at least 1 cm, and a W3C wiki summary of academic studies reports a 20 mm optimum for non-disabled users and continued gains to 30 mm for users with motor disabilities (original papers not opened) ([W3C wiki](https://www.w3.org/WAI/GL/mobile-a11y-tf/wiki/Summary_of_Research_on_Touch/Pointer_Target_Size)). So the skill's 28px is the macOS default (DOC), above the AA floor and not a touch value, exactly as `erp.md` half-says; the gap is a coarse-pointer value. Hoober's field data (49% one-handed, 36% cradled, 15% two-handed among 780 observed touches in public places, 2013) is an observational study of people in transit that the author later corrected, so it supports "design for one thumb" but not a warehouse reach map ([UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)).

**Tables on small screens.** SAP's responsive table hides columns by `minScreenWidth` or pops them into a row-detail area by `importance`, requires at least one column to stay visible, and calls tables "one of the biggest challenges in responsive web design" ([UI5 table docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAP-docs/sapui5/main/docs/10_More_About_Controls/configuring-responsive-behavior-of-a-table-38855e0.md)). NN/g recommends sticky headers, a locked first column, a visible scroll cue and user-chosen columns, but states it has no quantitative data ([NN/g mobile tables](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/)). Ant offers `fixed` columns, `sticky` and per-column `responsive` breakpoints ([Ant Table](https://ant.design/components/table/)). WCAG 1.4.10 requires reflow at 320 CSS px without two-dimensional scrolling but excepts "parts of the content which require two-dimensional layout for usage or meaning", with data tables named in the Understanding text, while each cell still has to reflow ([Understanding 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)). No empirical comparison of card transformation, horizontal scroll and pop-in was found, so the skill should offer them as three documented patterns, state that none is evidence-ranked, and keep the sticky header and first column it already has. The marketing comparison table's "phone: two columns with a picker, not sideways scroll" has the same status as NN/g's two-column suggestion for wordy comparison tables.

**List-detail, navigation, dashboards.** Material shows both panes at Expanded width and one pane with a back path below it ([canonical layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/canonical-layouts)); SAP's Flexible Column Layout uses three columns at 1280 and up, two at 960-1280, one below 960, with a 312px minimum column ([FlexibleColumnLayout.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAP/openui5/master/src/sap.f/src/sap/f/FlexibleColumnLayout.js)); Material also says compact height (phone landscape) makes two panes impractical even at medium width. The thresholds disagree (SAP shows one pane on a 900px tablet, Material shows two), so the skill should give the collapse rule and name thresholds as per-system choices. For navigation, Material pairs a bottom bar of three to five destinations with a rail of three to seven and a permanent drawer as an override ([BottomNavigation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-components-android/master/docs/components/BottomNavigation.md), [NavigationRail](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-components-android/master/docs/components/NavigationRail.md)); the HIG says to prefer a sidebar or adaptive tab bar for complex structure and to avoid overflow "More" tabs ([HIG tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars.json)). The one quantitative study is NN/g's n = 179 test on six consumer sites: hidden navigation cut content discoverability by more than 20% and made desktop tasks at least 39% slower, mobile 15% slower ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)); it did not test ERP, and the 3-5 and 3-7 limits are component rules, not research. Few's single-screen dashboard is expert argument, not experiment ([Perceptual Edge](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/Whitepapers/Dashboard_Design.pdf)). The skill's 52px topbar, 260px sidebar, 320-360px inspector and 44px toolbar are asserted values; keep them as defaults and label them so.

**Forms and input.** Label-above and a single column have consistent support: NN/g (citing a CHI 2014 study in which guideline-following forms had 78% one-try error-free submission against 42%, paper not opened) ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/)), Penzo's small ad hoc eye-tracking study ([UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2006/07/label-placement-in-forms.php)), and Baymard's checkout testing, which also names exceptions for associated fields (name parts, city/state/ZIP, card number/expiry/security code) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/avoid-multi-column-forms)). That is MODERATE for e-commerce checkout and should not be stretched to dense ERP entry; Baymard's own exception and the grouping findings suggest grouped multi-field rows are fine. `erp.md`'s "validate on blur" rests on one 22-person study showing validation after leaving a field was 7-10 s faster than "while" or "before and while" in the first half of the form, with a 22% success-rate gain for the best inline version ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/inline-validation-in-web-forms/)); label it that way. On mobile, Baymard's benchmark of 48 sites found 54% fail to use all relevant purpose-built keyboards and that the numeric keyboard's keys are 521% larger on an iPhone 6S ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-touch-keyboards)); MDN documents `inputmode` values and that it enforces no validity ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Global_attributes/inputmode)). Barcode and camera input, wizards versus long forms and money-field error prevention have no source in the notes.

**Poor connectivity and remote sites.** Microsoft documents an offline-first pattern for field staff: changes are stored on the device and uploaded automatically, offline setup is recommended even when connectivity looks reliable, and limits are explicit (under 200,000 records, 100 tables, 1 GB data, 4 GB files and images; the first sync "can take time ... especially in remote areas") ([Field Service offline profile](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/field-service/mobile-power-app-system-offline), [mobile offline overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/mobile/mobile-offline-overview), [optimize the profile](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/mobile/mobile-offline-guidelines)). SAP's offline OData queues and replays requests in order and surfaces upload errors only after upload, which differs from Microsoft's automatic conflict handling ([SAP tutorial](https://developers.sap.com/tutorials/fiori-ios-hcpms-offline-odata-introduction.html)). NN/g's loading rule (skeleton for full-page loads under 10 s, spinner for single modules, a progress bar above 10 s with an explicit estimate) and the 0.1/1/10 s limits are IND, and one skeleton-versus-spinner test of 136 people that found no perceived-time benefit was seen only in a snippet ([NN/g skeletons](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/), [response times](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Core Web Vitals give the measurable limits: LCP 2.5 s, INP 200 ms, CLS 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)); `erp.md`'s "reserve space, never animate layout on data arrival" matches the CLS definition. Mobile is where pages struggle: the 2025 Web Almanac puts good Core Web Vitals at 48% of mobile sites against 56% on desktop, with median Total Blocking Time 1,916 ms on mobile against 92 ms on desktop ([Web Almanac performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)). Evidence about devices is general-web, not ERP: in October 2026 StatCounter shows Indonesia 58.37% desktop and 41.13% mobile, with 360x800 the most common phone size at 8.7% ([StatCounter Indonesia](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile-tablet/indonesia), [Indonesia mobile](https://gs.statcounter.com/screen-resolution-stats/mobile/indonesia)); it measures page views across StatCounter's sites, not intranets, and no ERP-specific mobile share was found. No source gives an offline indicator pattern, an optimistic-UI pattern or an evidence base for sync-status layout, so those must be labelled design judgement.

**Field conditions the notes could not verify.** Glove sizes (MIL-STD-1472 touch zones of 15 mm bare and 20 mm gloved), DTIC spacing, an ISO 9241-411 reference, a 5:1 ambient-contrast target for sunlight and a mining or plant ERP study all failed verification: the sources were search snippets or returned 403, so none may become a number in the skill. Hoober's 2017 targets (7 mm at centre, about 12 mm at corners, each capturing about 95% of observed taps) are practitioner synthesis. The honest design response is a declared-conditions step: ask whether users wear gloves, work outdoors, on shared handsets, on 2G/3G or satellite links, and then apply the verified floors (coarse-pointer 44-48 px with 8 px spacing, text at 7:1 where AAA is cheap, offline-first per Microsoft's documented limits) while saying that the glove and sunlight values are unsourced and must be field-tested. The 7:1 choice for sunlight is an inference from WCAG's AAA tier and Apple's "strive for 7:1" text, not a finding.

**Accessibility rules that bind layout.** WCAG 2.2 adds items the skill's sticky stack must respect: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA) fails when a sticky header, toolbar or footer entirely hides a focused control, and scroll padding is the named remedy ([Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)); 2.5.7 requires a non-drag alternative to drag operations; 3.2.6 and 3.3.7 (both Level A) require consistent help placement and no re-entry of information already given in the same process; 1.3.4 forbids locking orientation unless essential; 1.4.4 requires 200% resize and 1.4.10 reflow at 320 px. Focus order should reinforce the visual reading order and DOM order should match visual order (2.4.3, 1.3.2). These apply to internal tools: the U.S. Revised 508 Standards cover ICT "procured, developed, maintained, or used" by agencies including intranet content, but incorporate WCAG 2.0 and apply only to U.S. federal agencies ([Access Board](https://www.access-board.gov/ict/)), and EN 301 549 v3.2.1 reflects WCAG 2.1 ([ETSI](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/03.02.01_60/en_301549v030201p.pdf)); whether the European Accessibility Act covers internal ERP was not checked.

**What outcome evidence exists.** Almost none. The one academic study with numbers compared two existing mobile ERP systems with 19 participants (effectiveness 77% against 63%, errors 23% against 37%, 1.72 minutes faster), so it says nothing about making a screen responsive ([Wüllerich and Dobhan 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/104450/104450.pdf)). "Fiori is 64% faster than SAP GUI" appears only on vendor marketing pages without method, and a trade-press warehouse figure (50% fewer pick errors) is a search snippet; both are excluded. The skill must not promise performance gains from its ERP layout.

## Colour: keep the contrast contract, demote the invented rules, add the missing roles

**The normative core.** WCAG 1.4.3 (AA) sets 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large-scale text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold, about 24px and 18.5px); 1.4.11 sets 3:1 for UI components, states and meaningful graphics; 1.4.1 forbids colour as the only carrier; ratios are thresholds that must not be rounded (4.499 fails); and `#767676` on white is 4.54:1 while `#777777` is 4.48:1 (own computation) ([Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html), [Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)). The 4.5 figure is 3:1 times 1.5 for roughly 20/40 acuity, a rationale rather than an experiment; the 3:1 base comes from ISO 9241-3 and ANSI-HFES 100-1988, neither of which the notes could open. The skill should present 4.5:1 as a floor, build ladders with margin (the notes suggest at least 4.6:1) and state that no rule says higher is better or worse, since 1.4.8 AAA only asks that users can choose their own colours. The audit's "large" definition (24px, or 18.66px and bold) matches the Understanding page's conversion, which `dark.md` leaves unstated. Focus: 2.4.7 (AA) requires only a visible indicator, in practice 1.4.11 forces 3:1 against adjacent colours, and the AAA criterion 2.4.13 asks for an area at least that of a 2px perimeter and 3:1 between focused and unfocused pixels ([Understanding 2.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html)); apple.com ships a 2px `#0071e3` ring with 3px offset (OBS), so the ERP rule's 2px offset is a house value. The skill has no non-text contrast rule; its dotnav resting dots at about 3.04:1 and 3.13:1 and disabled `opacity:0.42` clear or are exempt, but nothing checks them.

**Beyond the ratio.** The WCAG 2 ratio is the only normative method. APCA is a non-normative proposal; WCAG 3's draft (10 September 2026) says "the contrast algorithm ... is yet to be determined", APCA was removed from the draft in 2023 and its author says nobody should drop WCAG 2 conformance on its basis ([WCAG 3 WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0/), [Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/04/wcag3-contrast-as-of-april-2026.html)). MDN confirms the practical limit that 4.5:1 does not guarantee readability for mid-tone backgrounds (`#2277d3` is 4.52:1 against white and 4.65:1 against black, so both pass, own computation) ([MDN contrast-color()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/contrast-color)). The skill should keep the ratio as the pass/fail contract, add margin for near-black dark-mode pairs and mid-tone accents, and treat Lc values as non-normative if mentioned at all. Real systems turn contrast into structure: USWDS says a grade difference of 50 or more meets AA, 70 or more AAA, and grade 50 passes against both white and black ([USWDS color](https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-tokens/color/overview/)); Stripe built its palette in CIELAB so that five levels apart guarantees small-text contrast and four apart icons and large text, after an audit found none of its default small-text colours except black met 4.5:1 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems)); Radix guarantees steps 11 and 12 at Lc 60 and 90 on step 2 ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)); Material expresses role pairs as tone-delta curves such as `ContrastCurve(4.5, 7, 11, 21)` for on-surface ([color_spec_2021.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/main/typescript/dynamiccolor/color_spec_2021.ts)); and GitHub's Primer runs a script across every token in every theme on each pull request after auditing more than 100 pairs ([GitHub blog](https://github.blog/2023-05-11-unlocking-inclusive-design-how-primers-color-system-is-making-github-com-more-inclusive)). The common pattern is a two-layer model (tonal scale, then semantic roles) with contrast checked by script in CI, which is what the skill's audit should do for the roles it defines, in every theme it defines. WebAIM Million 2026 finds low-contrast text on 83.9% of home pages and the 2025 Web Almanac finds 30% of sites meeting contrast ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/projects/million/), [Web Almanac accessibility](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/accessibility)), so shipping a failing default token (as `--ink-muted` does) reproduces the industry's most common defect.

**Polarity, dark mode and "pure black".** Controlled studies find a positive-polarity (dark text on light) advantage for acuity and proofreading that grows as characters shrink (Piepenbrock 2013, 2014), while Dobres et al. 2017 found the advantage in dim ambient light but not in bright light; the cloudy-media benefit for dark mode rests on seven participants, and the myopia-marker study has n = 7 (all via NN/g, [NN/g dark mode](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)). NN/g's stance is not to default to dark for a general audience and to let users switch. The hype that dark mode is better for the eyes has no support, and astigmatism or halation claims have no primary source in the notes. Apple's HIG asks for dimmer backgrounds and brighter foregrounds, base and elevated backgrounds, a 4.5:1 minimum and a strive-for 7:1 for small text, and says nothing about pure black ([HIG Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)). Real dark backgrounds diverge: apple.com is `rgb(0,0,0)` with fills `rgb(22,22,23)` and `rgb(29,29,31)` (OBS), Carbon's darkest is `#161616` ([Carbon color](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/color/overview/)) and Material's dark surface is tone 6. So "pure black, not a dark grey" is Apple's choice and "never replace identity" is the better rule. There is no verified general-population penalty for `#000` on `#fff`; dyslexia studies are small and mixed (Rello and Baeza-Yates 2012: 23 dyslexic and 92 other participants, fastest black on cream; Rello and Bigham 2017: 341 participants, warm backgrounds beat cool) and favour user control over a baked tint ([W3C WAI RD](https://w3.org/WAI/RD/2012/text-customization/r11)). The 12:1 "ceiling" has no source; remove it or label it the author's preference. The skill should default small body text and dense data screens to dark-on-light, keep dark sections as a large-type or identity choice, and offer dark as an option through `prefers-color-scheme`, which is what the evidence supports.

**State colours, colour blindness and accent.** The skill's "8% of men" line should cite Birch 2012: about 8% of men and 0.4% of women of European descent, 4-6.5% of Chinese and Japanese men ([Birch, Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:22472762%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json)). WCAG 1.4.1 already makes redundancy normative, Apple's HIG names red-green and blue-orange as difficult pairs and asks for shapes or icons in addition to colour, and Okabe and Ito's rules (vary shape and position, not only hue) are practitioner guidance ([Okabe and Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)). The red-amber-green-blue mapping is convention; the only empirical cross-cultural data is Jonauskaite 2020 (4,598 participants, 30 nations, average similarity r = .88 with national deviation beyond it), which supports roles with icon plus text, not "red means danger" ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/169496/)). `erp.md` has a good structure (badge carries the word, tint not fill) but no hex values in guidance, only in the example page, and "five hues" really means four chromatic ones. The skill should add values verified at 4.5:1 for text on the tint and 3:1 for the badge edge, in both schemes. On accent, the notes support one distinct accent against a quiet field as salience (Healey 1996: seven isoluminant colours as the maximum for rapid identification, with an informal "five to seven" guideline that has no explanation; Few's soft colours for most information and bright or dark for what needs attention) and refute "red buttons convert better": the cited Performable test has no sample size, is not peer reviewed, and vendors explain it as contrast with a green page ([Healey](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1996/TR-96-10_0.pdf), [Few 2008](https://www.makinggood.ac.nz/media/1257/few_2008_rules_for_using_color.pdf), [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/20566/the-button-color-a-b-test-red-beats-green.aspx)). The 60-30-10 rule and "blue means trust" have no supporting source in the notes; Cyr, Head and Larios 2010 found colour appeal drives trust and satisfaction with cultural differences ([McMaster](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/58866)), and Reinecke and Gajos 2014 (2.4 million ratings, nearly 40 thousand participants) found preferences vary by demographic ([Harvard](https://eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2014/reinecke14visual.shtml)). That is evidence for the skill's "keep the site's identity" rule; label the 7-colour cap as being about data encodings needing fast identification, not decorative palettes.

**Colour spaces, preferences and data colour.** For building tonal ramps, perceptual spaces beat HSL: Ottosson's Oklab fixes CIELAB's hue-prediction problem in blue (hue error 0.49 against 0.69) but was fit to CAM16 data, so it is an engineering model, not a measured truth ([Ottosson](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)). `oklch()` is at 95.31% global support, `color-mix()` 95.02%, and relative colour syntax 93.83% ([caniuse oklch](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_types_color_oklch), [color-mix](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_types_color_color-mix), [relative colors](https://caniuse.com/css-relative-colors)), but CSS Color 4 is a Candidate Recommendation Draft and Level 5 a Working Draft, so `color-mix` and `contrast-color()` rest on drafts ([CSS Color 4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/), [CSS Color 5](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-5/)). Display P3 should be progressive enhancement over an sRGB fallback verified at the same ratios (Apple: P3 colours "appear fine on sRGB displays" but similar P3 colours can be hard to distinguish and gradients can clip) ([HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). `prefers-contrast` is Baseline widely available since May 2022 and `forced-colors` since September 2022; MDN says authors should use forced-colors only for small tweaks ([MDN prefers-contrast](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-contrast), [MDN forced-colors](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors)). Adoption is low (`prefers-color-scheme` around 13% of pages, `prefers-contrast` around 1%, `forced-colors` 16-19% in 2025), which argues for the skill modelling them as first-class. For charts and heatmaps the notes support ColorBrewer's sequential, diverging and qualitative types, single-hue lightness-ordered sequential scales, diverging only around a meaningful midpoint, no red-green pair, and non-data ink kept light ([ColorBrewer](https://colorbrewer2.org/learnmore/schemes_full.html), Few 2008). The Okabe-Ito hex values were only on a secondary blog, so do not hard-code them. The "greyscale-first" workflow is convention; the harness lists a separate `dataviz` skill, which the skill could point to rather than duplicate. Reading text over photographs has no numeric source: WCAG measures contrast against the rendered background, gives no gradient method and exempts text that is part of a picture with significant other visual content (not hero headlines), and Few's "avoid gradients behind text" was written for charts. The skill's three scrim recipes (`transparent → 0.25 at 35% → 0.68`, an opaque-to-58% fade, and the showcase's) are unreconciled and unsourced. The defensible check is the notes' own inference: measure the worst-case background region under the text and justify a scrim by raising that to the target ratio, a CSS-controlled scrim being measurable.

## Generating images, video and interactive visuals from a brief when Claude cannot paint pixels

**What exists today and what is missing.** The skill sources existing public-domain or licensed photography (Wikimedia Commons, Pexels, Unsplash, Openverse), builds six-file video ladders and start stills with `make-clip.sh` (ffmpeg and `cwebp`), and renders deterministic HTML scenes to video through headless Chrome and CDP screenshots with `render-clip.mjs`. Its honesty rule says generated imagery, if used at all, is limited to abstract backdrops and disclosed in `CREDITS.md`. A grep for SVG, Canvas, WebGL, Three, Lottie, GSAP, Rive, model-viewer, WebM, alpha, AV1 and "brief" found nothing relevant: there is no brief intake, no brief-to-art-direction step, no inline SVG or Canvas guidance, no scrub-encode script (`make-clip.sh` hard-codes `-g 300`), no alpha video, no AVIF generation, no external generation integration and no provenance handling. Its accessibility handling stops at reduced motion and accessible names; it has no 2.2.2 pause control for loops, no flash check and no captions policy. Claude's vision documentation is explicit: "Claude is an image understanding model only. It can interpret and analyze images, but it cannot generate, produce, edit, manipulate, or create images" ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). Writing SVG, HTML or Canvas as text is code output and is not covered either way by that page.

**What the evidence says imagery should be.** The independent evidence supports real, informative pictures, not decorative ones. NN/g's 2010 eye-tracking study (participant count not given) found people spent 10% more time on real team portraits than on much longer bios, studied detailed product photos and ignored generic filler; Baymard's current benchmark (155+ sites) says 37% of sites lack in-scale images, 23% lack human-model images, 42% of users judge size from images and 56% of test subjects look at product images before titles or descriptions, and its 2019 video study found 41% of users watched product videos, one third had trouble with tabs separating video and image thumbnails, and 35% of sites do not embed video where users look ([NN/g photos](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/), [Baymard product page UX](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux), [Baymard video](https://baymard.com/blog/embedding-product-page-videos)); the 28% against 37% in-scale figures differ across articles and the later one should be cited with that caveat. GOV.UK says not to use images for decoration and to avoid stock photos meant to convey mood ([GOV.UK images](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/images/)). No independent large-sample study links hero video, product video, image count or the skill's 3-second clip to conversion, and "18x engagement" style numbers are vendor blogs. The skill's 38% media share and 3:1 media-to-text ratio are Apple's rates (REPO), not effectiveness evidence, and its subject hierarchy (real photograph, real screenshot, render, abstract field, illustration, icon) is consistent with NN/g and GOV.UK but is one organisation's policy. NN/g's auto-forwarding carousel case is n = 1 ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding/)).

**The route ladder for a brief.** The notes support a five-route ladder, in this order of trust, and each route has its own limits.

Real assets (client photos, packshots, screenshots, licensed stock) | Selection, crops, resizing, WebP/AVIF, art direction per breakpoint | Needed for company profiles (people, premises, products) and product pages; no code or model route reproduces a specific real product, person or place (inference). So text that matters stays HTML (also required by WCAG 1.4.5 and GOV.UK). Model names and prices must be checked each run, not hard-coded in the skill. Documented MCP servers for image generation exist but their maintenance and licences were not checked.

**Legal, trust and accessibility constraints.** The U.S. Copyright Office's January 2025 report says copyright "does not extend to purely AI-generated material", prompts alone do not give enough control, and human selection, arrangement and creative modification can qualify ([USCO Part 2](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)); that is U.S. guidance, case by case. Indonesian law was seen only in secondary reporting (human authors only, draft revision under consultation) and must stay UNVERIFIED. C2PA provenance is tamper-evident but can be separated from the asset when metadata is stripped ([C2PA 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)); only Google's SynthID watermark was confirmed among providers. For consumer protection and AI-disclosure effects, no regulator text was opened, and the SAGE and conference-poster findings (disclosure raises perceived novelty and lowers perceived authenticity; an apparel purchase-intent drop of 0.499) are snippets: do not state them as findings. What is safe to say is the skill's own honesty rule, plus recording tool, date and human edits in `CREDITS.md` as a provenance habit consistent with the Copyright Office's disclosure expectation. On accessibility, 1.1.1 requires text alternatives (decorative content ignorable), 1.2.1 an alternative for video-only content, 2.2.2 a pause, stop or hide mechanism for content that starts automatically and lasts over five seconds, 2.3.1 no more than three flashes per second, and parallax is named under 2.3.3 (AAA) with `prefers-reduced-motion` as technique C39 ([Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html), [Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). The skill's play-once 3-second silent clips sit under the five-second threshold; any looping or ambient clip does not, and `prefers-reduced-data` has no browser support at all in the Baseline data, so it cannot be relied on.

**Performance bounds.** LCP candidates include `<img>`, a `<video>` poster or first frame, and `url()` backgrounds; the LCP image should be discoverable early, given `fetchpriority="high"` (one or two images at most) and never lazy-loaded; 9.5% of LCP images on mobile in 2024 were lazy-loaded ([web.dev LCP](https://web.dev/articles/lcp), [optimize LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp), [Web Almanac 2024 Media](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/media)). The median 2025 mobile home page carries 911 KB of images in 2,559 KB, so the skill's 250 KB hero plus 260 KB clip budgets are large shares of a typical page ([Web Almanac page weight](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight)). AVIF reached Baseline high on 2026-07-25 and WebP in 2023, so the prose recommendation for AVIF now has support but no tool. Scroll-scrubbed video has only practitioner evidence: one test showed a 5-frame keyframe interval costing 845 KB (MP4) against 146 KB at 100 frames, with Firefox choppy on MP4, which agrees with the repo's "3-5x" claim; canvas image sequences from numbered stills are how third-party write-ups say Apple does it (observed by others, UNVERIFIED) ([muffinman.io](https://muffinman.io/blog/scrubbing-videos-using-javascript/)).

**Brief to art direction.** The only citable material is commercial brief templates (background, objectives, audience, one key message, deliverables, tone adjectives, mandatories, timeline, approvals) and single-organisation policies from GOV.UK and IBM that prefer real, fact-based imagery to mood imagery. No tested method maps brand attributes to visual attributes, and no evidence on mood-board effectiveness exists. The intake the skill adds is therefore professional convention, to be labelled as such: audience, one message, tone adjectives with guardrails, real assets available, legal mandatories, what must be photographed versus what can be drawn, the page's performance and accessibility budget, and whether the user holds generation API keys.

## Fifteen changes to make, in priority order, with the evidence behind each

The first group needs no new research; every item is an arithmetic or consistency fix already established by two independent computations. Later groups are ordered by evidence strength, with normative items before industry practice before convention.

## Conclusion

The research turns the skill's identity from "Apple's measurements" into a layered contract. At the base sit normative checks that a script can enforce and that two independent computations already show the skill failing on its own tokens. Above those sit documented vendor patterns, which tell the skill what real systems do on phones, tablets and large desktops and show that "below 1069px is a different product" is one author's decision against everyone else's documentation. At the top sit Apple's craft choices (the em rhythm, the 28px radius, the 3-second clips), which can stay as the skill's flavour once they are labelled as that. The sharpest practical shift is for ERP work: density and target size should follow the pointer, layout should follow the container, and field conditions should be asked for and tested, because the notes found no public evidence that would let a skill assert glove, sunlight or offline-indicator numbers.

For media, the most useful insight is that Claude's inability to paint pixels is a routing problem, not a dead end: most company-profile and e-commerce value sits in real people, premises and products that no generator can supply, while the abstract, diagrammatic and software-UI visuals that code can author are exactly the ones the evidence says are fine to fake. The remaining uncertainty is wide. Baymard and NN/g give behaviour anchors but not conversion proof, generation-model terms and prices changed within months, legal and disclosure effects were mostly snippet-level, and no study measures responsive ERP layouts in field conditions. A skill that says so, labels every rule by evidence type and tests the rest, will age better than one that keeps asserting measurement.
