## Controls, retention law and the common errors they prevent

Indonesian law requires bookkeeping in good faith, in Indonesia, in Latin letters, Arabic numerals, Rupiah and Indonesian, recording assets, liabilities, capital, income, costs, sales and purchases, with books, records and supporting documents (including electronic data) kept 10 years at the place of business or corporate seat (UU KUP Pasal 28 as rendered by [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-28-tahun-2007/pasal-28); the DDTC quote of Pasal 28(11) is a snippet, [DDTC](https://news.ddtc.co.id/literasi/kelas-pajak/16241/kewajiban-pembukuan-dan-pencatatan)). UU 8/1997 independently defines "catatan" to include annual statements, accounts and "jurnal transaksi harian", requires Rupiah and Indonesian, and keeps financial records 10 years from the end of the fiscal year (Pasal 5, 8, 11) while Pasal 10(1) requires the annual statements on paper and Pasal 10(2) lets accounts and journals be kept on other media ([UU 8/1997](https://peraturan.go.id/files/uu8-1997.pdf), LAW, read in full). Because the daily journal is itself a retained legal record, deleting posted entries is both a control failure and a retention problem (inference). Whether later electronic-transaction laws changed the paper requirement, and whether cloud hosting outside Indonesia conflicts with the in-Indonesia rule, were not researched and need counsel.

The ledger controls the notes derive are: balanced entries per entry (OpenStax 3.5); no edits or deletes of posted rows, with corrections by reversal and re-post; unique sequential numbering with gap detection; an audit trail retained 10 years; segregation of duties and maker-checker ([OpenStax 8.2](https://openstax.org/books/principles-financial-accounting/pages/8-2-define-and-explain-internal-controls-and-their-purpose-within-an-organization)); period locks; a Rupiah amount on every line; and an exact-decimal money type. **Only the first and the Rupiah and retention items have a legal or standard source; gapless numbering, the audit-log shape, maker-checker thresholds and period-lock behaviour are engineering controls** and the notes found no Indonesian accounting law requiring gapless journal numbers (Faktur Pajak numbering is a separate tax rule). COSO's framework (1992, updated 2013, five components) is cited only for existence; the component and principle names were not read from COSO ([COSO](https://www.coso.org/guidance-on-ic)), so any list from memory is UNVERIFIED. The common bugs, each tied to the rule they violate in the notes, are: treating debit as increase; checking balance only on the trial balance; storing running balances without journal lines; recording cash movement as the transaction in an accrual ledger; editing posted entries; using floats for money; a single signed amount with no sign convention; posting directly to control accounts; leaving revenue or expense balances after closing; retranslating non-monetary items; skipping period-end remeasurement of monetary items; netting receivables against payables without permission (IAS 1.32); using old PSAK numbers; assuming one framework; and putting the KMK tax rate in the accounting FX field. A validator list of eight checks follows from these (inference): balanced in transaction and functional currency; account active and postable; period open; mandatory dimensions present; unique document number; approver different from maker; no update or delete on posted rows; FX rate source and date recorded.
