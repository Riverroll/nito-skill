# How real ERPs implement the ledger, and what MariaDB/MySQL can enforce

> Part of the `nito-accounting` skill. Text is taken from the research report dated 16 November 2026 (`research/erp-accounting-implementation-guide.md` in this repo). Every claim keeps its evidence label; see `evidence-labels.md`. Worked entries are constructed illustrations: amounts invented, rules sourced. Rates, caps and deadlines live in `values-to-verify.md` and must be re-checked before use.

## How real ERPs implement the ledger, and what MariaDB can and cannot enforce

### Ledger shape, money types and the posting layer

All compared systems use a header-and-line model or a voucher-keyed line table, with a base-currency amount and a foreign-currency amount on every line. Odoo 17 stores `account.move` and `account.move.line`, with a signed `balance` from which `debit` and `credit` are derived in the company currency, plus `amount_currency` and `currency_id`, and a SQL constraint `check_credit_debit` (credit times debit equals zero) so a line is one side only ([Odoo account_move_line.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/account/models/account_move_line.py), CODE). ERPNext's GL Entry keeps separate debit and credit plus account-currency and transaction-currency columns, a `voucher_type`, `voucher_no` and `voucher_detail_no` source link, and an `is_cancelled` flag ([ERPNext gl_entry.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/erpnext/version-15/erpnext/accounts/doctype/gl_entry/gl_entry.json), CODE). Oracle Fusion's `GL_JE_LINES` has one-to-many headers to lines, separate entered and accounted debit and credit columns and a conversion rate ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/financials/25d/oedmf/gljelines-20303.html), VENDOR). Oracle's `GL_JE_HEADERS` columns, SAP's BKPF/ACDOCA fields and the schemas of Tryton, OFBiz, GnuCash and NetSuite were **not verified**. SAP's Universal Journal is known only from SAP Learning and a SAP PRESS blog: ACDOCA combines general ledger, controlling, asset accounting and the material ledger, and BKPF/BSEG still exist for open-item management ([SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/outlining-cost-management-and-profitability-analysis/discussing-the-concept-of-the-universal-journal_f62cf151-5562-44f5-8ff6-df16fb232e94); [SAP PRESS](https://blog.sap-press.com/what-is-saps-universal-journal)), with the claim that aggregate tables were removed resting on search snippets (UNVERIFIED against help.sap.com).

Posting is a separate layer. Oracle's Subledger Accounting turns an accounting event plus transaction data and rules (journal-line, description and account rules inside an accounting method) into subledger journal entries, then summarises or details them into the GL; "transaction objects need to be populated before the accounting for the events occurs" ([Oracle Accounting Hub guide](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E36909_01/fusionapps.1111/e20374/F484500AN2530D.htm), VENDOR). Dynamics 365 Finance builds subledger journal entries from posting profiles or priority-ordered posting definitions, requires balanced debits and credits, transfers to the ledger in detail or summary, and uses the voucher as the shared reference ([Dynamics ledger-subledger](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/finance/general-ledger/ledger-subledger); [posting definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/finance/general-ledger/posting-definitions), VENDOR). ERPNext funnels everything through `make_gl_entries`, which handles dimension offsets, period and disabled-account checks, merging of similar entries, and a debit-credit difference check that either raises "Debit and Credit not equal" or posts a small difference to a Round Off account ([general_ledger.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/erpnext/version-15/erpnext/accounts/general_ledger.py), CODE); its Company doctype carries more than twenty default-account fields (bank, receivable, payable, round-off, write-off, realised and unrealised FX, inventory, stock adjustment, accumulated depreciation, CWIP, advances, deferred revenue and expense, and others) ([company.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/erpnext/version-15/erpnext/setup/doctype/company/company.json)). Odoo's l10n_id chart template sets property accounts (receivable `l10n_id_11210010`, payable `l10n_id_21100010`, stock accounts), FX gain and loss accounts, 8-digit codes and default taxes `tax_ST4` and `tax_PT4` ([template_id.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/l10n_id/models/template_id.py), CODE). Account type drives behaviour rather than the number: Odoo's `account_type` keys (asset_receivable, liability_payable, equity_unaffected and others) set report placement and the `reconcile` flag, and ERPNext's `root_type`, `report_type` and `account_type` do the same ([account_account.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/account/models/account_account.py); [account.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/erpnext/version-15/erpnext/accounts/doctype/account/account.json)).

Money types differ, and the notes flag that. DECIMAL is MySQL's documented exact type for monetary data; ISO 4217 gives IDR an exponent of 2 (JPY 0, KWD 3) ([MySQL 8.4](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/fixed-point-types.html); [SIX list-one](https://www.six-group.com/dam/download/financial-information/data-center/iso-currrency/lists/list-one.xml)); that the sen is "no longer used in practice" is from Indonesian Wikipedia only, with no Bank Indonesia source ([id.wikipedia](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupiah)). TigerBeetle uses unsigned 128-bit integer minor units ([TigerBeetle](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle/main/docs/coding/data-modeling.md)); Odoo's `stock.valuation.layer.unit_cost` is a Float ([stock_valuation_layer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/stock_account/models/stock_valuation_layer.py)); Prisma maps `Decimal` to decimal.js with a MySQL default of DECIMAL(65,30) ([Prisma](https://www.prisma.io/docs/orm/reference/prisma-schema-reference)), and decimal.js defaults to 20 significant digits and ROUND_HALF_UP ([decimal.js](https://mikemcl.github.io/decimal.js/)). Floats fail because 0.1 has no finite binary representation, with a documented single-precision compound-interest error of 1.40 dollars ([Goldberg](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E19957-01/806-3568/ncg_goldberg.html)) and JavaScript's safe integer limit is 2^53 - 1 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Number/MAX_SAFE_INTEGER)). Both Odoo (ROUND(SUM) to the currency's decimal places) and ERPNext (small differences to a Round Off account) resolve line-versus-total drift with a rounding tolerance and a dedicated account, which supports the design inference of one rounding point. That `new Prisma.Decimal(24.454545)` from a JS number inherits float imprecision is not in the Prisma docs and is an inference; construct from strings. Fowler's `allocate` method and Dinero.js allocation were not verified.

### Immutability, numbering, locking and reconciliation in practice

Odoo enforces balance in Python: `_check_balanced` runs SQL grouped by move with `HAVING ROUND(SUM(line.balance), decimal_places) != 0` inside create and write ([Odoo account_move.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/account/models/account_move.py), CODE). It uses states draft, posted and cancel, blocks deleting posted lines, reverses rather than deletes entries that cannot be unlinked, and offers an optional SHA-256 hash chain per journal over name, date, journal, company and lines with a `secure_sequence_number`, which becomes mandatory for French companies where cancellation and modification methods are deactivated ([Odoo France docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/17.0/applications/finance/fiscal_localizations/france.html), VENDOR). ERPNext keeps originals and posts matching reversals on cancellation; the setting name "Enable Immutable Ledger" comes only from a search snippet ([ERPNext](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/immutable-ledger-in-erpnext)). The high-scale published ledgers agree: Square Books uses only inserts and corrects by new entries with balances cached on entries ([Square](https://developer.squareup.com/blog/books-an-immutable-double-entry-accounting-database-service/)); Modern Treasury keeps posted entries permanent and balances derived from aggregate fields with optimistic `lock_version` ([Part II](https://moderntreasury.com/journal/how-to-scale-a-ledger-part-ii); [optimistic locking](https://moderntreasury.com/journal/designing-ledgers-with-optimistic-locking)); Stripe's Ledger is an immutable event log, uses clearing accounts that must net to zero as a completeness test and requires two-phase review for corrections ([Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/ledger-stripe-system-for-tracking-and-validating-money-movement)); Helland's "Accountants don't use erasers" gives the principle ([CIDR 2015](https://www.cidrdb.org/cidr2015/Papers/CIDR15_Paper16.pdf)); Fowler's Accounting Transaction sums to zero and prefers Reversal Adjustment when entries are immutable ([Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingTransaction.html)). No source documents the anti-pattern of deriving the GL from operational tables at report time, and the notes found no property-based test suite for ledgers.

Numbering is where the systems and the owner's stack diverge most. Odoo numbers per journal using a max-plus-one scan with retry on a PostgreSQL unique violation, backed by a **partial unique index** on `(name, journal_id)` for posted entries, and by its own code it does not guarantee gapless numbers ([sequence_mixin.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/account/models/sequence_mixin.py), CODE). Both MySQL AUTO_INCREMENT and PostgreSQL sequences leave gaps after rollback ([MySQL](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/innodb-auto-increment-handling.html); [PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/functions-sequence.html)). Period locks are application hooks everywhere (Odoo `_check_fiscalyear_lock_date` with optional irreversible `account_lock`; ERPNext Accounting Period and Accounts Frozen Upto; [Odoo year-end](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/17.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/year_end.html), [ERPNext Accounting Period](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/accounting-period)), with LedgerSMB's PostgreSQL trigger `prevent_closed_transactions` the only database-level lock found ([triggers.sql](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ledgersmb/LedgerSMB/master/sql/modules/triggers.sql)). Closing models differ: Odoo computes reports in real time and treats the closing entry as optional with an `equity_unaffected` account type, whereas ERPNext's Period Closing Voucher moves profit and loss to equity but "does not erase history or stop backdated entries" ([ERPNext PCV](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/period-closing-voucher)). Balance storage has three documented strategies: computed from lines (Odoo), a per-period summary table (Oracle's `GL_BALANCES` with begin balance and period net debit and credit) and cached running balances (Square, Modern Treasury, TigerBeetle) ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/financials/25d/oedmf/glbalances-25110.html)), and any cache is a derived copy of the lines that needs a rebuild job and a reconciliation to SUM(lines) (inference from Helland).

Reconciliation structures differ by design. Odoo matches open items with `account.partial.reconcile` (debit and credit move, amount, `full_reconcile_id`, `exchange_move_id`) ([account_partial_reconcile.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py)); ERPNext uses a Payment Ledger Entry ([payment_ledger_entry.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/erpnext/version-15/erpnext/accounts/doctype/payment_ledger_entry/payment_ledger_entry.json)) and a Stock Ledger Entry with `valuation_rate`, `stock_value_difference` and a FIFO `stock_queue` ([stock_ledger_entry.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/erpnext/version-15/erpnext/stock/doctype/stock_ledger_entry/stock_ledger_entry.json)); ERPNext's goods-received-not-invoiced account is "Stock Received But Not Billed", credited at Purchase Receipt and debited by Purchase Invoice ([ERPNext perpetual inventory](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/perpetual-inventory)). Multi-currency follows the same pattern: Odoo posts exchange differences to a dedicated journal and accounts ([Odoo multi-currency](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/17.0/applications/finance/accounting/get_started/multi_currency.html)), ERPNext runs an Exchange Rate Revaluation that needs an unrealised gain/loss account and supports reversal journals ([ERPNext](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/exchange-rate-revaluation)); where Odoo implements unrealised revaluation was not located. On Indonesia, Odoo 17 splits the localisation into `l10n_id` (chart and taxes), `l10n_id_efaktur` (CSV export and a 13-digit number format) and a master-branch `l10n_id_efaktur_coretax` module ([Odoo Indonesia docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/17.0/applications/finance/fiscal_localizations/indonesia.html)), and DJP's Coretax import uses XML with converters (Faktur Keluaran converter v1.6 dated 23 January 2026, field names unverified) ([DJP](https://www.pajak.go.id/reformdjp/coretax/template-xml-dan-converter-excel-ke-xml)). ERPNext's `id_chart_of_accounts.json` exists among 76 verified charts but its contents were not opened, and Frappe's marketing claim of PPN, PPh, e-Faktur and BPJS support is unverified.

### The enforcement boundary for MariaDB, MySQL and Prisma

| Rule | Can the database enforce it? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Exact money values | Yes, with DECIMAL(p,s) | [MySQL fixed-point](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/fixed-point-types.html) |
| A line is debit or credit, never both; non-negative amounts | Yes in principle: a row-level CHECK on the same row (Odoo's `credit * debit = 0` is the model); enforcement version on MySQL 8.0.x and MariaDB behaviour not confirmed | [MySQL 8.4 CHECK](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/create-table-check-constraints.html); [MariaDB constraint](https://mariadb.com/kb/en/constraint/) |
| Sum of debits equals sum of credits per entry | **No.** CHECK is single-row, "Subqueries are not permitted", other tables not allowed; only NDB defers FK checks | MySQL 8.4 CHECK and [FK](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/create-table-foreign-keys.html) pages |
| Same enforcement via triggers | **Limited.** A trigger cannot modify a table already used by the invoking statement; SIGNAL is permitted; MariaDB trigger behaviour not opened | [MySQL stored-program restrictions](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/stored-program-restrictions.html) |
| Gapless document numbers | **No with AUTO_INCREMENT** (gaps after rollback, not reused); yes with a locked counter row via `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` in the posting transaction | [MySQL AUTO_INCREMENT](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/innodb-auto-increment-handling.html); [locking reads](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/innodb-locking-reads.html) |
| Unique number per journal and company | Yes with a unique index; Odoo's partial unique index (posted only) is a PostgreSQL feature and MySQL partial-index absence was not verified in docs | [Odoo account_move.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/odoo/odoo/17.0/addons/account/models/account_move.py) |
| Immutability of posted rows | Not documented as a DB feature on MySQL; Odoo, ERPNext, Square and Modern Treasury do it in application code or by never issuing UPDATE; a DB trigger guard is a possible design but is untested in the notes | CODE and ENG sources above |
| Period lock | Not at DB level except LedgerSMB's PostgreSQL trigger; other systems use application hooks | [LedgerSMB](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ledgersmb/LedgerSMB/master/sql/modules/triggers.sql) |
| Referential integrity of lines to header and accounts | Yes, with FKs (InnoDB checks immediately). Partitioned InnoDB tables cannot have FKs, so partitioning a ledger table conflicts with FKs | [MySQL partitioning limits](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/partitioning-limitations-storage-engines.html) |
| Atomicity and isolation | Yes: transactions; InnoDB default REPEATABLE READ; under READ COMMITTED locking reads lock only index records, so phantoms can occur | [MySQL isolation](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/innodb-transaction-isolation-levels.html) |

Prisma's `$transaction` accepts an `isolationLevel` (default is the database's), an interactive-transaction `timeout` defaulting to 5 seconds and `maxWait` of 2 seconds ([Prisma reference](https://www.prisma.io/docs/orm/reference/prisma-client-reference)); how it behaves on MariaDB beyond these options was not verified. The synthesis the notes support, and which is **a design inference** from the MySQL, Odoo and ERPNext sources and not a documented pattern of any named system, is a single `$transaction` that (a) locks a per-journal counter row with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`, (b) inserts header and lines with DECIMAL amounts, (c) asserts that the line sum is zero in the same transaction and in both transaction and functional currency, and (d) marks the entry posted, followed by a verification query and a scheduled job asserting `SUM(debit) = SUM(credit)` globally. Idempotency should come from a client-generated key stored uniquely, as in Stripe (keys can be pruned after at least 24 hours, parameters compared on retry; [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests)), TigerBeetle's transfer `id` and the transactional-outbox pattern whose relay may publish more than once, so consumers must be idempotent ([microservices.io](https://microservices.io/patterns/data/transactional-outbox.html)).
